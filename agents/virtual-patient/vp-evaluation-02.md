# Virtual Patient 2

## Prompt

Given the case:

-----

NURSE:
Doctor, we have a man (51 years old) who entered the emergency department reporting chest pain. His vital signs are ABP: 144x92mmHG; HR: 78bpm; RR: 21rpm; Temp: 37oC; O2Sat: 98%.

PATIENT:
Doctor, I am feeling chest pain since yesterday. The pain is continuous and is located just in the middle of my chest, worsening when I breathe and when I lay down on my bed. I suffer from arterial hypertension and smoke 20 cigarettes every day. My father had a “heart attack” at my age and I am very worried about it.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: The cardiac and pulmonary auscultation are normal; chest pain does not worse with palpation of the thorax; there is no jugular stasis nor lower limb edema.

-----

Do you think myocardial infarction is a good diagnostic hypothesis for this case? Could you list the elements that support my hypothesis and those that contradict it? Don't suggest other hypotheses so I can think about my own.

## Answer

Based on the information provided, **myocardial infarction (MI) is a reasonable diagnostic hypothesis to consider for this patient.**

Here's a breakdown of the elements supporting this hypothesis and those that contradict it:

**Elements Supporting Myocardial Infarction:**

*   **Chest Pain:** The patient is presenting with chest pain, which is a hallmark symptom of MI.
*   **Pain Characteristics:** The pain is described as continuous, located in the middle of the chest, and worsening with breathing (pleuritic component) and lying down. While typical MI pain is often described as pressure or tightness, atypical presentations are common, especially in older patients or those with comorbidities. Worsening with breathing can sometimes suggest pericarditis, but it can also occur with MI, particularly if there's associated inflammation or involvement of the pleura. Worsening when lying down is also a common feature of cardiac pain.
*   **Risk Factors:** The patient has significant risk factors for MI:
    *   **Age:** 51 years old (risk increases with age).
    *   **Arterial Hypertension:** A major risk factor for coronary artery disease (CAD) and MI.
    *   **Smoking:** 20 cigarettes per day is a significant risk factor for CAD and MI.
    *   **Family History:** A father who had a "heart attack" at a relatively young age (51) increases the patient's risk.
*   **Patient Anxiety:** The patient's expressed worry ("I am very worried about it") is common in individuals experiencing chest pain, especially when they have risk factors and a family history.

**Elements Contradicting Myocardial Infarction (or suggesting other possibilities):**

*   **Normal Physical Examination:**
    *   **Normal Cardiac Auscultation:** While a normal heart sound doesn't rule out MI, significant ST-elevation MI often presents with signs of heart failure (S3 gallop) or arrhythmias. A normal exam might suggest a non-ST-elevation MI (NSTEMI) or an unstable angina, but it doesn't exclude MI entirely.
    *   **Normal Pulmonary Auscultation:** This makes significant pulmonary edema (often seen in large MIs with heart failure) less likely, but doesn't rule out MI.
    *   **No Chest Wall Tenderness:** The pain does not worsen with palpation, making musculoskeletal causes (like costochondritis) less likely, although MI pain can sometimes be exacerbated by movement or palpation.
    *   **No Jugular Stasis or Lower Limb Edema:** These signs are typically associated with right-sided heart failure, which can be a complication of MI, but their absence doesn't rule out MI itself.
*   **Vital Signs:**
    *   **Heart Rate (HR): 78 bpm:** This is within the normal range. While tachycardia can occur during an MI, a normal heart rate doesn't exclude it.
    *   **Respiratory Rate (RR): 21 rpm:** This is slightly elevated (normal is typically 12-20), which could indicate anxiety or early respiratory distress, but is not a specific sign of MI.
    *   **Blood Pressure (ABP): 144/92 mmHg:** This is elevated, consistent with his history of hypertension, but not necessarily indicative of an acute MI (though hypertension can be a risk factor).
    *   **Temperature: 37°C:** Normal temperature.
    *   **O2 Saturation: 98%:** Normal oxygen saturation.

**Conclusion:**

While the physical examination findings are reassuring and don't point strongly towards a classic MI presentation, the patient's symptoms (chest pain characteristics), significant risk factors (age, hypertension, smoking, family history), and expressed anxiety make **myocardial infarction a plausible and important diagnostic hypothesis** that needs to be actively investigated. The normal physical exam might suggest a less severe form of MI (like NSTEMI or unstable angina) or potentially another cardiac or non-cardiac cause of chest pain. Further investigations (ECG, cardiac enzymes, imaging) are crucial.
