# Virtual Patient 3

## Prompt

Given the case:

-----

NURSE:
Doctor, we have a man (51 years old) who entered the emergency department reporting chest pain. His vital signs are ABP: 144x92mmHG; HR: 78bpm; RR: 21rpm; Temp: 37oC; O2Sat: 98%.

PATIENT:
Doctor, I am feeling chest pain since yesterday. The pain is continuous and is located just in the middle of my chest, worsening when I breathe and when I lay down on my bed. I suffer from arterial hypertension and smoke 20 cigarettes every day. My father had a “heart attack” at my age and I am very worried about it.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: The cardiac and pulmonary auscultation are normal; chest pain does not worse with palpation of the thorax; there is no jugular stasis nor lower limb edema.

-----

The diagnosis is myopericarditis. Consider a student who answered myocardial infarction; what elements would you give this student to reflect on his answer? Do not state the correct diagnosis.

## Answer

Here are some elements a student who answered myocardial infarction (MI) could reflect on to improve their answer:

1.  **Patient History:**
    *   **Risk Factors:** While the patient has risk factors for MI (age, smoking, hypertension, family history), the *nature* of the pain described (worsening with breathing and lying down) is less typical for classic MI pain, which is often described as pressure, squeezing, or tightness and is usually *not* significantly worsened by breathing or lying down.
    *   **Family History:** While family history is a risk factor, the specific detail about the father having a "heart attack" at the same age might suggest a familial predisposition to other conditions, potentially including pericarditis, although it doesn't rule out MI.
    *   **Duration:** The pain started "yesterday" and is "continuous." While MI can present acutely, the continuous nature and specific positional worsening might point away from a typical acute MI presentation.

2.  **Symptoms:**
    *   **Pain Characteristics:** The description of the pain (middle of the chest, continuous, worsening with breathing and lying down) is more characteristic of pericarditis than typical MI pain. MI pain is often described as pressure, squeezing, or tightness, and while it can radiate, it's less commonly exacerbated by breathing or lying down in the way described.
    *   **Associated Symptoms:** The prompt doesn't mention other common MI symptoms like shortness of breath (dyspnea), diaphoresis (sweating), nausea, or vomiting, although these aren't always present.

3.  **Physical Examination:**
    *   **Auscultation:** The prompt states cardiac and pulmonary auscultation are normal. While a normal auscultation doesn't rule out MI, specific findings like S4 gallop, new murmurs, or signs of heart failure (crackles) might be expected in some MI cases. Importantly, the *absence* of specific pericardial friction rub (though not mentioned, it's a key sign of pericarditis) is noted.
    *   **Other Findings:** The absence of jugular venous distension (JVD) or lower limb edema suggests no significant right-sided heart failure, which can occur with extensive MI.

4.  **Differential Diagnosis Considerations:**
    *   **Pericarditis:** The pain characteristics (worsening with breathing and lying down) are highly suggestive of pericarditis. The patient's risk factors (smoking) can contribute to inflammation.
    *   **Other Causes:** Other causes of chest pain like musculoskeletal pain, gastrointestinal issues (GERD, esophageal spasm), or pulmonary embolism should also be considered, but the specific features point more towards pericarditis.

**In summary, the student should reflect on:**

*   **How the specific characteristics of the patient's pain (worsening with breathing and lying down) align more closely with pericarditis than typical MI pain.**
*   **How the patient's risk factors, while relevant, don't fully explain the pain's specific nature.**
*   **The importance of considering the full clinical picture, including symptom characteristics and physical exam findings, when differentiating between potential diagnoses.**
*   **The possibility that the patient's presentation is not typical for MI and might represent another condition, such as pericarditis.**
